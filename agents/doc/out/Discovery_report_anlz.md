# Analysis of the Discovery evaluation report, well 15/9-19 SR

Source: `Discovery_report.pdf`, "Discovery Evaluation Report, Well 15/9-19 SR,
Theta Vest Structure, PL 046", Statoil, December 1993, Volume 1, 194 scanned
pages, English. Conversion: `data/volve/reports/md/Discovery_report.md`. Page
numbers below are PDF pages. Numbers quoted here are in the fact register,
`data/facts/disc1993.csv`.

The structure the report calls Theta Vest is the one later developed as
Volve. The report gives percentiles as P(90), mean and P(10); **P(90) is the
low value**.

The same three questions were put to this report as to the development plan.
Two of them do not apply, because the report evaluates a discovery; it does
not plan a development.

## 1. Concept selection

**Not covered.** The report contains no development concept and no
alternatives. The only statement about development is the original purpose of
the well: had it found hydrocarbons in its primary target, the Heimdal
Formation, it was to be completed as a producer and tied to the Loke subsea
well 15/9-C2H (p16). The Heimdal Formation was water-bearing; oil was found in
the secondary target, the Hugin Formation.

## 2. Production strategy and forecasts

**Not covered.** The report has no production strategy, no production
forecast and no production profile, so there is no plot data to export. What
it does contain is the evidence on productivity that later plans build on.

*Well 15/9-19 SR, Hugin Formation (Tables 6.2 and 7.1, p107 and p110)*

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Reservoir thickness in the well | 18 m (2 864–2 882 m below sea level), oil-filled to its base |
| Net/gross; porosity; water saturation | 0.924; 22.6 %; 11.0 % |
| Permeability from logs | 2 913 mD |
| Drill-stem test, maximum oil rate | 1 358 Sm3/d |
| Test permeability | 914 mD |
| Productivity index | 144 Sm3/d per bar |
| Reservoir pressure; temperature | 327.7 bar; 106.2 °C |

The test indicates "very good production capacities" (p110). It also shows
pressure barriers about 60 m and 250 m from the well and an increase in flow
capacity about 125 m away (p110, p113).

The recovery factor used for the resource estimate, 0.45 with a range of 0.35
to 0.50, is "purely based on the test results". The report notes that mapped
faults, some with throws large enough to break communication, could give a
lower factor (p174–175).

### The uncertainties the report identifies

| Uncertainty | What the report says | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Fluid contact** | No contact could be determined from the well. The base case assumes the structure is filled to its spill point; the estimate "is based on the assumption that the oil-water contact is governed by the spill point" | p15; p173–174 |
| **Fault seal** | Faults cutting the crest could be sealing. If they are, fault segments north, west and south of the structure can be hydrocarbon-bearing, which "would increase the resource potential of the area by several orders of magnitude". The same question decides whether oil reached the eastern segment (probability 0.9) | p14–15; p175 |
| **Reservoir thickness** | The well found 18 m, in a zone of intense faulting (829 fractures in the cores); the unfaulted thickness is estimated at 24–27 m, and the reservoir "probably thickens downflanks" | p14; p172–173 |
| **Seismic quality** | The top Hugin horizon was "difficult to interpret consistently" because of limited resolution and a weak reflector; mapping of facies and thickness of the Hugin Formation in the area is "very uncertain" | p14–15 |
| Gas cap | Possible from the pressure response, but judged unlikely because the oil is undersaturated (bubble point 273 bar, reservoir pressure 327.7 bar) | p173 |
| Reservoir distribution | The Hugin sandstones are identified in only three wells in the area | p15 |
| Recovery | Could be lower than 0.45 if faults break communication | p174 |

### What the report recommends

A re-evaluation in two stages: first on existing data, then after new 3D
seismic (to be acquired in 1994) and after two appraisal or exploration wells
in the Theta Vest area planned for 1995–1997 (p190–191).

## 4. From this report to the development plan

The same structure, twelve years apart. Values from the fact register.

| Oil, million Sm3 | Discovery report, 1993 | Development plan, 2005 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| In place, P90 / mean / P10 | 7.22 / 10.33 / 12.83 | 20.9 / 27.5 / 35.2 | Mean 2.7 times higher |
| Recoverable, P90 / mean / P10 | 3.18 / 4.55 / 5.68 | 9.0 / 11.40 / 15.31 | Mean 2.5 times higher |
| Recovery factor, most likely | 0.45 | about 0.43 | |

- **The two ranges do not overlap.** The 1993 high case for oil in place
  (12.83) is below the 2005 low case (20.9).
- **What changed in between,** according to the development plan: appraisal
  well 15/9-19A "proved a much thicker reservoir than the discovery well", and
  the field was remapped on ocean-bottom seismic (PDO p7, p13). Thickness and
  seismic quality are two of the uncertainties this report names.
- **The contact question stayed open.** In 1993 no contact had been found and
  the spill point was assumed; in 2005 still no well had met a contact, and
  the contact was the largest uncertainty in the plan.
- **Scope.** The 1993 estimate is for the Theta Vest structure as mapped
  then, filled to a spill point at 2 945 m. The 2005 estimate is for the
  "Volve closure" with a contact at 3 120 m in the base case. Whether the two
  outlines are the same has not been established.

## 5. How this analysis was made

- The report is a scan. Each page was transcribed from its image, with the
  machine-read text as an aid. Pages that are figures, tables or mixed are
  kept as images beside the Markdown.
- Table 10.1 was checked digit by digit against the scan. The other tables
  quoted here were read once and are marked medium confidence in the fact
  register.
- Not done: Volume 2 of the report (19 enclosures: maps and logs) is not in
  the dataset. Values were not read off the report's plots, maps or logs.
