Oil production

The plan expected a year of build-up, 19 months at a plateau of 9 000 Sm3/d, and a decline to the economic limit in May 2012.

  • Later. First oil came on 12 February 2008, not 6 March 2007. The plan itself warned that weather during tow-out could delay the start.
  • Lower early on. The best calendar year gave 2.72 million Sm3 (2009) against 3.10 planned. The highest monthly average was 8 924 Sm3/d, close to the plateau rate, but it was not sustained.
  • Longer. 2.4 million Sm3 was produced after month 63 of production, the point at which the plan expected to stop.
Counted from first oil, production was 6.3 million Sm3 in the first 36 months against 8.1 planned (−23 %), and 7.7 against 11.4 in the first 60 months (−32 %).

How the estimate of recoverable oil moved

Sodir publishes an estimate of recoverable oil for every field at each year-end. For Volve it rose before first oil, fell below the plan's low case after two years of production, and recovered after the second drilling campaign.

Water and gas

The plan relied on water injection to hold the reservoir pressure above the bubble point, so that all gas would come out of solution at the surface, not in the reservoir.

Over the field's life 30.3 million Sm3 of water was injected and 15.3 million Sm3 produced. In the plan's producing years, 2007 to 2012, the digitised plan curve amounts to 25.1 million Sm3 injected; the outcome for 2008 to 2012 was 17.8.

Gas produced was 1.48 billion Sm3 against 1.5 planned. Gas sold was 0.81 billion Sm3 against 1.34 planned as dry gas; the two sales figures may not be defined alike.

Cost

Only investments can be compared: Sodir publishes investments per field and year, and no operating or lease costs. In the plan, investments were about a quarter of the total cost, because the platform and the storage vessel were leased.

  • Initial development. 2 933 million NOK was invested from 2005 to 2009 against 2 043 planned (+44 %).
  • Second campaign. A further 1 640 million NOK was invested in 2012 to 2014, when new wells were drilled. The plan contained no such campaign; it listed new wells as a possible way to increase recovery.
  • In 2005 money. Deflated with the consumer price index, the outcome is 4 327 million NOK against 1 968 in the plan (+120 %). The index is a general one, not a cost index for the oil industry, so this is indicative.

Context

Oil price

The plan's economic limit, and with it the field's planned life, was set by an expected Brent price of 22 USD per barrel. Its break-even price was 17.50 USD per barrel.

Oil in place

The volume the plan was built on was later revised down by about a third. The recovery factor moved the other way: 41 % in the plan's mean case, 54 % in Sodir's final figures.

What this comparison does not show

  • Operating and lease costs. No public source for the actual figures was found, so about three quarters of the planned cost has no counterpart here.
  • Like-for-like investments. It is not established that Sodir's investment figures cover the same items as the plan's. The plan treats the capital element of the leases as an operating cost.
  • Causes. The comparison shows what differed, not why. The reasons for the lower early production are not taken from any document read for this report.
  • Precision of the plan curves. Rates for oil, water and gas are digitised from small pictures in the PDO. Annual oil is checked against the plan's Table 4.5 and agrees within about 1 %, except 2010 (5 %).
  • Two production records. Sodir gives 10.17 million Sm3 of oil; the released production workbook sums to 10.04. The difference is not explained.
